# Project rules and deliverables
Type: normative · Version: v3 (2026-10-07) · Source: Lesson 1 (L1) slides 38–51; Lesson 2 (L2) slides 108–110; syllabus (Syl) p. 4

## Rules (L1 s43–44, s51)
- Topics come from the teaching team's catalogue; own proposals: in general no. Students form their groups, which rank 3 preferences; the team assigns one, respecting the ranking where possible.
- Each team has at least one designer and one engineer (no designers-only). Solo: in general no; for major-force reasons (health, family), contact the teaching team. Exchange students plan their timing so as not to stress the group.
- Equal effort from all; all members attend every tutoring (tracked). Unbalanced participation → a meeting; at the end, members may get different scores.

## Activities and milestones (L1 s38–39, s45–46)
- Understand stakeholders' needs → state of the art → design, prototype, evaluate → document → present. Each tutoring has results due, per the schedule.
- Demo Madness: every group pitches (+ demo), early December, half day, mandatory; feedback from peers, teachers, tutors, external stakeholders.
- Final presentation + delivery at an official exam date (dates TBA); any session, winter (Jan–Feb) STRONGLY RECOMMENDED. Erasmus/double-degree: present BEFORE going home.

## Deliverables on the exam date (L1 s47; L2 s108)
1 Project report (professional quality; must include the UNG model and scenarios); 2 presentation (ppt); 3 prototype with source code; 4 video pitch (max 2 min); 5 complementary material, if any. The syllabus (p4) also lists video scenarios, a user manual, and slides for the 20-min final presentation.
Empirical evaluation (L1 s48–49): optional in some projects, mandatory in others; at any stage, with representatives of the stakeholders; if done, its report is included.

## Report structure (L2 s109–110)
1 Cover + abstract · 2 Team · 3 Member contribution + table of contents (page numbers) · 4 Executive summary · 5 Requirements · 6 State of the art · 7 UX design · 8 Implementation · 9 Empirical evaluation · 10 Value proposition · 11 Discussion, future work · 12 Business (optional) · Bibliography (ACM/IEEE) · Annexes.
Sections the tracked deliverables cover (others: ask the teaching team):
- §5: per the UNG model – stakeholders, needs, context, constraints, goals. Needs are properties of the users; goals are properties of the system, more concrete than needs, and say what, not how.
- §6: related research works, projects and market products.
- §7: general approach (max 1 page: paradigms, content, main tasks); user workflow diagrams; ALL relevant scenarios (sub-scenarios if needed); dialogue diagrams for conversational projects.
